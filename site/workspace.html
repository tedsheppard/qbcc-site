Adjudication application – ABC Subcontractor Pty Ltd v DEF Head Contractor Pty LtdClaimant’s submissions
D.The payment schedule was not given within time
The chronology
41.The claimant gave Payment Claim 14 to the respondent at 3.47 pm on Tuesday, 3 March 2026, by email to the address nominated in item 22 of the Contract Particulars (Trevane SD at [18]–[21]; exhibit MT-7).
42.The Contract does not fix a period for giving a payment schedule shorter than the period allowed by the Act. The respondent was therefore required to give a payment schedule within 15 business days after the payment claim was given: s 76(1) of the Act.
43.The fifteenth business day after 3 March 2026 was Tuesday, 24 March 2026. No day in that period is excluded from the definition of “business day” in schedule 2 of the Act.
44.The respondent’s document headed “Payment Schedule – PC14” was sent by email at 6.12 pm on Wednesday, 25 March 2026 (Trevane SD at [24]; exhibit MT-9). It was given on the sixteenth business day, one day out of time.
The “draft assessment” of 24 March 2026 was not a payment schedule
45.The claimant anticipates that the respondent will contend that the email sent by its contract administrator at 5.58 pm on 24 March 2026, attaching a spreadsheet headed “PC14 – draft assessment (subject to review)”, was a payment schedule given within time (Trevane SD at [23]; exhibit MT-8).
46.It was not. A payment schedule must identify the payment claim to which it responds and state the amount of the payment, if any, that the respondent proposes to make: s 69 of the Act. The draft assessment:
(a)did not identify Payment Claim 14, referring only to “the March claim”;
(b)did not state any amount the respondent proposed to pay, its final line being left blank; and
(c)was expressly provisional, being “subject to review and certification by the Superintendent”.
47.A document that leaves open the amount the respondent proposes to pay does not tell the claimant what is in dispute, which is the purpose a payment schedule serves under the Act. It is not saved by having been sent within time.
Consequences
48.Because no payment schedule was given within time:
(a)the respondent became liable to pay the claimed amount of $412,860.00 on the due date for the progress payment: s 77 of the Act;
(b)the respondent having also failed to pay any part of that amount by the due date, the claimant was entitled to apply for adjudication: s 79 of the Act; and
(c)the respondent is not entitled to give an adjudication response: s 82 of the Act.
49.In the alternative, if the adjudicator finds that the draft assessment was a payment schedule, the respondent is confined to the reasons for withholding payment stated in it. Those reasons are answered in Part E below.

How do SopalAI credits work

Credits are used when SopalAI does work for you: answering a question, researching, and drafting. A short answer uses a fraction of a credit; a researched draft of a full section uses more. Each user gets 100 credits a month. Uploading, Boolean search, reading documents, editing and exporting to Word don’t use credits.

PAYG credits

Questions

How do we get started?

Create an account for your firm and add a card. Nothing is charged for the first month. Then drag in a folder or a zip of your past submissions and invite your colleagues. Documents become searchable as soon as they’ve been read, so you can start searching while the rest are still being filed. A short checklist in the app walks you through the first search, question and draft.

What counts as a set of submissions?

One adjudication application, adjudication response or set of further submissions, together with anything in the same file, such as its annexures. Your firm’s first 500 are included in the subscription. After that, each additional set is $1.20 a month.

What files can we upload?

PDF and Word documents, one at a time, as a whole folder, or as a zip file of any size. Scanned PDFs are read too. Statutory declarations aren’t kept in the library, because they’re a witness’s own evidence rather than the firm’s work.

How are our submissions filed?

Each set is read and categorised automatically: the document type, the parties, the State, the amounts claimed and scheduled, and the date. You can correct any of these, and your correction is kept. If the same submission is uploaded twice, it’s flagged so the library doesn’t fill up with copies.

How is this different from searching our shared drive?

A shared drive searches file names, and at best finds a word. Sopal Workspace searches the full text of every page with Boolean and proximity searching, and SopalAI can answer a question across the whole library, showing you the paragraphs that answer it and where they came from.

How does SopalAI draft submissions?

Tell it what you need the way you would brief a junior, and name a precedent to follow if you like. It reads your firm’s precedents first, then the current legislation, Australian case law and past adjudication decisions on the point, and then writes. The draft appears in an editor with proper paragraph numbering, in your firm’s format, ready to edit or export to Word.

Can the AI get things wrong?

Yes. SopalAI is an AI model, so its work should be checked before it’s relied on or filed, the same as anyone else’s draft. Answers from your library show the paragraphs they came from, so you can check them quickly.

Who can see our documents, and are they used to train AI?

Only the people your firm invites can see your library. Documents are stored in Sydney. Sopal has special enterprise agreements with frontier model providers to ensure your documents are not used to train AI models.

How do credits work, and what happens when they run out?

Each user has 100 SopalAI credits a month, used when SopalAI answers questions, researches or drafts. Uploading, Boolean search, reading and exporting don’t use credits. Once a user’s 100 are used, work continues on the firm’s pay-as-you-go credits, which the owner or an admin buys in $50 steps or tops up automatically, up to a monthly limit they set.

What happens after the free month?

From then on it’s $140 a month for the first user and $90 a month for each additional user, GST included, charged to the card you added when you signed up. We’ll email you 5 days before your free month ends, and you can cancel before then and won’t be charged.

How does billing work when we add people?

Each person you invite gets their own free first month and their own 100 credits. Their $90 a month starts only once their free month is over, and is charged pro rata with the firm’s subscription. The owner and admins can see who is on the account, and download tax invoices, in Settings.

How do we cancel, and what happens to our library?

Your firm’s administrator can cancel at any time in Settings, including during the free month, and nothing more is charged. Everyone keeps full access until the end of the free month or the period already paid for. After that, the firm’s account, its library and every member’s account are closed and deleted, so download anything you want to keep first. If a colleague would like to keep using Sopal Workspace, contact us before cancelling and we can move the subscription to them, so everyone else keeps their account and their work.

Is this the same account as Sopal Decisions?

No. Sopal Workspace has its own accounts, separate from sopal.com.au. A Sopal Decisions subscription doesn’t include Workspace, and nothing is shared between the two.
