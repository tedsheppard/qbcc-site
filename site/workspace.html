Adjudication application – ABC Subcontractor Pty Ltd v DEF Head Contractor Pty LtdClaimant’s submissions
D.The payment schedule was not given within time
The chronology
41.The claimant gave Payment Claim 14 to the respondent at 3.47 pm on Tuesday, 3 March 2026, by email to the address nominated in item 22 of the Contract Particulars (Trevane SD at [18]–[21]; exhibit MT-7).
42.The Contract does not fix a period for giving a payment schedule shorter than the period allowed by the Act. The respondent was therefore required to give a payment schedule within 15 business days after the payment claim was given: s 76(1) of the Act.
43.The fifteenth business day after 3 March 2026 was Tuesday, 24 March 2026. No day in that period is excluded from the definition of “business day” in schedule 2 of the Act.
44.The respondent’s document headed “Payment Schedule – PC14” was sent by email at 6.12 pm on Wednesday, 25 March 2026 (Trevane SD at [24]; exhibit MT-9). It was given on the sixteenth business day, one day out of time.
The “draft assessment” of 24 March 2026 was not a payment schedule
45.The claimant anticipates that the respondent will contend that the email sent by its contract administrator at 5.58 pm on 24 March 2026, attaching a spreadsheet headed “PC14 – draft assessment (subject to review)”, was a payment schedule given within time (Trevane SD at [23]; exhibit MT-8).
46.It was not. A payment schedule must identify the payment claim to which it responds and state the amount of the payment, if any, that the respondent proposes to make: s 69 of the Act. The draft assessment:
(a)did not identify Payment Claim 14, referring only to “the March claim”;
(b)did not state any amount the respondent proposed to pay, its final line being left blank; and
(c)was expressly provisional, being “subject to review and certification by the Superintendent”.
47.A document that leaves open the amount the respondent proposes to pay does not tell the claimant what is in dispute, which is the purpose a payment schedule serves under the Act. It is not saved by having been sent within time.
Consequences
48.Because no payment schedule was given within time:
(a)the respondent became liable to pay the claimed amount of $412,860.00 on the due date for the progress payment: s 77 of the Act;
(b)the respondent having also failed to pay any part of that amount by the due date, the claimant was entitled to apply for adjudication: s 79 of the Act; and
(c)the respondent is not entitled to give an adjudication response: s 82 of the Act.
49.In the alternative, if the adjudicator finds that the draft assessment was a payment schedule, the respondent is confined to the reasons for withholding payment stated in it. Those reasons are answered in Part E below.

One library for everything the firm has filed

Drag in a folder or a zip file of past submissions. Each one is read, including scanned pages, and filed with its document type, the parties, the State, the amount claimed and the date. If something is read wrong you can correct it, and your correction is kept.

Adjudication applications, responses and further submissions all go in. PDF and Word files both work. If the same submission is uploaded twice it’s flagged, so the library doesn’t fill up with copies of the same document.

The library belongs to the firm, so it stays when people come and go.

Find how the firm argued a point last time

Boolean search works the way it does on the case law databases: AND, OR, NOT, phrases, truncation, and proximity such as w/10. It runs across every page of every submission in the library.

You can also ask SopalAI a question in plain English, for example which responses argued that a payment claim didn’t identify the work it related to. The answer lists the submissions that made the argument and cites the page each point came from. Any passage it quotes is checked against that page before it’s shown to you.

Click a citation and the page opens beside the answer, so you can read it in context.

Draft submissions drawing on your firm’s precedents

Computer mode takes an instruction the way you’d give it to a junior, such as “draft the part of our response on the late payment claim, using last year’s response for the same client as the model”.

It reads the firm’s precedents first. It then reads the relevant sections of the Act at source, the case law, and past adjudication decisions on the point, and only then writes. The draft follows the structure and depth of the firm’s own best work rather than a generic template.

The draft appears in an editor with proper paragraph numbering, so you can change it directly or ask for changes. When it’s ready, export it to Word.

How do SopalAI credits work

Credits are used when SopalAI does work for you: answering a question, researching, and drafting. A short answer uses a fraction of a credit; a researched draft of a full section uses more. Each user gets 100 credits a month. Uploading, Boolean search, reading documents, editing and exporting to Word don’t use credits.

PAYG credits

Once someone has used their 100 credits for the month, work continues on the firm’s pay-as-you-go credits: $50 buys 60 credits. Buy them when you need them, or turn on auto top-up, which buys $50 at a time (or an amount you choose) when the balance runs low, up to a monthly limit you set. The owner and admins manage this.

Up to 500 sets of submissions included

Your firm can store up to 500 sets of submissions at no extra cost. Beyond that, each additional set is $1.20 a month, charged once for the firm, not per user.

Questions

How do we get started?

Create a firm account, upload a folder or zip of past submissions, and invite colleagues. Documents become searchable as soon as they’ve been read, and you can start searching while the rest are still being filed.

What files can we upload?

PDF and Word documents, one at a time, as a folder, or as a zip file. Scanned PDFs are read too. Statutory declarations aren’t kept in the library, because they’re a witness’s own evidence rather than the firm’s work.

Can the AI get things wrong?

Yes, which is why it shows its sources. Answers from your library cite the page each point came from, and quoted passages are checked against that page. Drafts should be read and checked before they’re filed, the same as you would with anyone else’s draft.

What happens after the free month?

From then on it’s $140 a month for the first user and $90 a month for each additional user, charged to the card you added when you signed up. We’ll remind you 5 days before your free month ends, and you can cancel before then and won’t be charged. Each user you add later has their own free first month before their $90 starts.

Is this the same account as Sopal Decisions?

No. Sopal Workspace has its own accounts, separate from sopal.com.au. A Sopal Decisions subscription doesn’t include Workspace, and nothing is shared between the two.

Can we take our documents with us if we leave?

Yes. The documents you upload are yours, and you can download them at any time.
