Find the submissions we did a few years ago where we argued that a notice of delay under clause 34.1 was not a condition precedent to an extension of time.

Two of the firm’s submissions make that argument:

  1. Tamsley Glass v Orrindale Built (application, 2023). Clause 34.1 required notice within 7 days, but nothing in it made the extension conditional on notice; clause 34.3, by contrast, used express time-bar words.p 14
  2. Brenlock Civil v Ostrova Projects (further submissions, 2024). The Superintendent’s power to extend time “whether or not notice was given” is inconsistent with a condition precedent.p 6

PART 1 – INTRODUCTION
1.These are the claimant’s submissions in support of its adjudication application under s 79 of the Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 (Qld) (the Act).
2.The claimant, ABC Builder Pty Ltd, claims $1,184,500.00 under Payment Claim 11 for work on the King Street apartments.
PART 4 – EXTENSIONS OF TIME
4.1The Subcontract
3.Clause 34 of the Subcontract provides for extensions of time to the Date for Practical Completion.
4.2The delays
4.The Works were delayed by 37 days, by the late issue of structural drawings and by 11 days of inclement weather.
4.3The claimant’s notices
5.The claimant gave written notice of each delay, although notice of the drawing delay was given 9 days after the delay began.
4.4Notice under clause 34.1 is not a condition precedent
6.The respondent says that, because notice of the drawing delay was given 9 days after it began, clause 34.1 bars any extension of time for it.
7.Clause 34.1 does not say so. It requires notice “within 7 days”, but it does not make an extension of time conditional on that notice, nor provide that the entitlement is lost if notice is late.
8.Where the parties intended a time bar, they said so. Clause 34.3 provides that a claim for delay costs “is barred” unless made within 28 days. The absence of those words from clause 34.1 is telling:
(a)the parties are taken to have chosen their words deliberately; and
(b)a provision that forfeits an accrued entitlement is construed strictly, and is not implied where the contract does not express it.
9.Clause 34.4 empowers the Superintendent to extend time “whether or not the Subcontractor has given notice”. That power is inconsistent with notice being a condition precedent.
10.The late notice therefore does not disentitle the claimant to the extension of 37 days addressed in section 4.5 below.
4.4The extension claimed
6.The claimant is entitled to an extension of 37 days, and so the respondent is not entitled to liquidated damages.
4.5Quantum
7.The value of the variations claimed is set out in the schedule at Tab 8.
PART 5 – CONCLUSION
8.The adjudicator should determine the adjudicated amount to be $1,184,500.00.
